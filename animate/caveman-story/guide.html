The Caveman and the Third Stone

A Caveman wants a third counting stone. A stream stands in his way. He studies it, commits to a jump, retrieves the stone and returns to count his collection with enormous pride.

Develop the whole film: story and Scenes → articulated poses → keys, breakdowns and in-betweens → walk → jump → lip sync → effects and camera → final delivery.

64 guided stages. Each workbook contains a short explanation, production practice, a deliberate repair and an independent director condition. Read-aloud, summaries, glossary, checklists, examples, sentence frames and stretch are available through My supports.

WORKBOOK 01

Make a story the audience can read

Story beats, staging, scene construction and an animatic

Build a readable three-Scene animatic before polishing any movement.

WORKBOOK 02

Build a Caveman you can pose

Parts, symbols, pivots, hierarchy and readable silhouettes

Assemble an articulated character and demonstrate intentional joint movement.

WORKBOOK 03

Make the Caveman think, then point

Key poses, designed breakdowns, in-betweens, timing and spacing

Build a short acting phrase whose transition is designed rather than left to default interpolation.

WORKBOOK 04

Walk to the edge with weight

Contact, down, passing, up, cycles and planted feet

Create a readable walk cycle and stage it without obvious foot sliding.

WORKBOOK 05

Jump the stream with a designed arc

Anticipation, take-off, breakdowns, landing, overshoot and settle

Animate a full jump with readable weight and a controlled landing.

WORKBOOK 06

Give the Caveman a voice

Recording, Stream sound, four mouth poses and true hand swaps

Synchronize mouth and gesture changes to the actual recording and preserve the original Caveman identity.

WORKBOOK 07

Use effects and camera to tell the story

Raw-shape tweening, splash timing, sound hierarchy and a motivated push-in

Add one controlled effect and one justified camera move that support the Caveman’s action.

WORKBOOK 08

Finish a complete Caveman film

Scene continuity, critique, revision, independent direction and delivery

Deliver a coherent, playable film and an editable project with evidence of a purposeful revision.

Use the actual recording. The lip-sync workbook preserves the original four-mouth Single Frame method and three true hand swaps. The natural voice take and its cue timings are made as part of the lesson; the synthetic author test is identified separately.
