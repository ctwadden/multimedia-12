Stream sound supports timeline synchronization. Real cues must come from this take, not the old 1/25/49 control test. All words play in the intended order, the clip is not cut off and the cue sheet names actual observed frames. If the start is silent, confirm the sound key is at 2 and that enough frames exist. If scrubbing is unreliable, test playback and inspect the selected frame’s sound settings.

SLIDE 04 — Set manual Single Frame lip-sync keys  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-04.mp3)
Single Frame holds the selected internal drawing until the next main-timeline change. The selected object is Mouth_Poses, not Hand_1. Different main-timeline keys show different First values and corresponding visible mouths. If the mouth cycles automatically, choose Single Frame. If all cues show the same pose, check that you inserted main-timeline keys and changed the selected instance at each key.

SLIDE 05 — Repair a cause, then show the difference  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-05.mp3)
The repair tests listening and timing, not just control settings. Playback of the corrected phrase agrees with the recording and the cue sheet.

SLIDE 06 — Make the changed condition work  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-06.mp3)
Record a more doubtful or more triumphant reading of the same line. Retain the words, remeasure cues, and redesign the gesture and timing. Show one audible cue, its timeline frame and the selected mouth pose; then explain one deliberate hold.
