RECORDING SCRIPT: read each slide in your own voice, one MP3 per slide.

SLIDE 01 — Finish a complete Caveman film  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-01.mp3)
Deliver a coherent, playable film and an editable project with evidence of a purposeful revision. A finished film coordinates story, performance, motion, sound and editing. Completion includes opening the deliverables and checking what the audience actually receives.

SLIDE 02 — The idea behind the controls  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-02.mp3)
The working FLA preserves editability; the rendered movie carries the viewing experience. An editor timeline or successful export command does not prove that the movie plays correctly. Native author tests are separate from a student cold run and from school assessment registration. This package does not claim live progress synchronization.

SLIDE 03 — Check continuity with sound muted  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-03.mp3)
A coherent silent action makes the spoken ending more satisfying and reveals visual dependence on explanation. A new viewer can recount what happened without you narrating over the film. If the return cut is confusing, add a small departure/arrival cue instead of a lengthy explanatory title.

SLIDE 04 — Run a focused audience review  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-04.mp3)
Targeted feedback reveals communication problems that familiarity can hide. The revision responds to observed audience difficulty, not only personal preference. If feedback is vague, ask which moment caused it; do not tell the viewer what they were supposed to think.

SLIDE 05 — Repair a cause, then show the difference  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-05.mp3)
The delivered files, not the workspace state, are what the audience and teacher can use. Both final files open and agree on the selected version, duration and ending.

SLIDE 06 — Make the changed condition work  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-06.mp3)
Choose one director condition from the previous workbooks and carry it consistently through the final film. Keep the shared story spine and explain the resulting choices. Show a before/after revision, identify the principle involved, and demonstrate that your final editable and playable files agree.
