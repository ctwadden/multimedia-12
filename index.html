Decode the impossible.

Look behind polished ads, learn the six moves that make a composite believable, and build a phone portal. Then make the illusion break its frame with the classic out-of-bounds effect.

Two-stage target: Tier 1 builds the portal. Tier 2 adds the butterfly with a refined, editable mask.

Big question: When is photo manipulation creative communication—and when can it become deception?

Launch: Project the gallery full-screen before students open Photoshop. Ask for silent noticing first, then pair talk. Do not explain the tools until students have made guesses.

Part 2 · Technique decoder

Six moves behind most composites.

Photoshop has many tools, but beginners can read a surprising amount of professional work by looking for the same small set of decisions.

The composite formula

LAYERS
separate pieces
+
MASKS
hide, don’t erase
+
MATCH
light, scale, colour

Believability test: edges + perspective + light direction + shadow softness + colour temperature.

V

Move

Positions a selected layer. If the wrong thing moves, select the correct layer first.

L / W

Select

Defines where an edit can happen. Marching ants are a boundary, not content.

mask

Layer mask

White reveals; black conceals. Reversible beats deleting pixels.

⌘/Ctrl T

Transform

Scales, rotates, or distorts one layer. Hold the corner and watch proportion.

T / U

Type + shape

Adds editable words and graphic accents that build hierarchy.

adj.

Adjust

Changes tone and colour non-destructively, often clipped to one layer.

Where do you finish today?

Tier 1 · Foundations

Complete Steps 0–10. Match the portal exemplar and prove that phone, forest, mask, adjustments, type, and accent remain editable.

Tier 2 · Out of Bounds

Continue to Steps 11–17. Cut out the butterfly, refine its mask, and place part of it beyond the phone’s edge.

Tier 3 · Transfer

After Tier 2, replace the butterfly with your own rights-safe subject. Keep every layer editable and defend the placement, edge, and shadow choices.

Pacing checkpoint: At the halfway mark, everyone should have a forest layer above the phone. Pair students by checkpoint rather than by speed. If selection time expands, provide Tier 1 with permission to use a simple rectangular screen selection and continue—the learning target is the mask workflow.

Part 6 · Retrieval check

Can you explain the build?

Answer without looking back first. Then check your score and repair any gaps.

1. Which feature hides pixels without deleting them?

Answer: Layer mask. White reveals; black conceals.

2. The wrong object moves when you press V. What should you check first?

Answer: The selected layer. Most “wrong thing moved” problems are layer-selection problems.

3. Why save a PSD before exporting a JPG?

Answer: PSD preserves layers, masks, type, shapes, and adjustments for revision.

4. Which pair most strongly affects whether a composite feels believable?

Answer: Perspective and lighting. Edges, shadows, and colour also matter.

5. What does a clipping mask on Curves accomplish here?

Answer: It limits Curves to the forest rather than changing the whole composite.

6. When can photo manipulation become deceptive?

Answer: Manipulation becomes ethically risky when it misrepresents subjects, products, evidence, or context.

7. Which action makes one butterfly edge appear to pass behind the phone bezel?

Answer: Black on the subject’s layer mask conceals only that small section and reveals the phone underneath.

8. The butterfly disappears when its layer is moved below the phone. What principle explains this?

Answer: Higher layers appear in front of lower layers; the phone can cover a subject placed beneath it.
