# Decode the Impossible — teacher notes

## Before class

- Open the live index, Parts 1–4, gallery, simulator, assignment and ZIP.
- Download the ZIP once and confirm every promised asset opens.
- Put the student URL in Google Classroom; HTML is the primary delivery format.
- Use the teacher brief for exact launch language and misconceptions.

## Three-class route

### Class 1: Diagnose, select and construct

- 0–8: Run a six-image diagnosis sprint: students rank the most believable composite and mark the first visual tell in the weakest one.
- 8–20: Explain: A composite begins with compatible viewpoint, resolution and light. Masks control visibility; transform and distort make the inserted object inherit a believable physical role.
- 20–35: Model the first three named steps and stop after each expected result.
- 35–60: Guided production/inquiry; use the warning and repair fields.
- 60–67: Checkpoint: Concept thumbnail, compatibility gate, v01 PSD and a rough-composite screenshot with the Layers panel are saved.
- 67–70: Exit: Which source mismatch would be most expensive to repair later, and why?

### Class 2: Make the impossible believable

- 0–8: Toggle the supplied good and bad exemplars for five seconds. Students point to the first lighting or shadow contradiction they notice.
- 8–20: Explain: The inserted Moon becomes a cause, not decoration. Nearby surfaces must receive its light; shadows must project away; local adjustments must respect form and material.
- 20–35: Model the first three named steps and stop after each expected result.
- 35–60: Guided production/inquiry; use the warning and repair fields.
- 60–67: Checkpoint: Midpoint screenshot shows named chair isolation, contact shadow, cast shadow, global grade and at least two local moonlight layers.
- 67–70: Exit: Which layer makes the Moon feel physically connected to the chair, and what evidence proves it?

### Class 3: Communicate, test and transfer

- 0–8: Show the final image without explanation. Reviewers write the perceived message, first focal point and least believable cue in five seconds.
- 8–20: Explain: Technical integration removes distractions; hierarchy and message determine meaning. A strong final revision targets the weakest system rather than adding more effects.
- 20–35: Model the first three named steps and stop after each expected result.
- 35–60: Guided production/inquiry; use the warning and repair fields.
- 60–67: Checkpoint: Final campaign, before/after, layered PSD, source log, critique evidence and production defence are complete; student can perform one live adjustment.
- 67–70: Exit: What changed after feedback, and why did that revision matter more than adding another effect?

## Assessment boundary

The website records practice only. Confirm achievement by inspecting the required evidence, asking the defence questions and using the rubric.
