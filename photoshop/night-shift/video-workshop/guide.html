PRODUCTION CONNECTION

What a compositor is solving.

Different photographs must appear to share a camera, a surface and a light source. Judge alignment, scale, edges, focus, value and colour. A saved stack is useful because clients request changes; the edit needs to survive revision.

MUST UNDERSTAND

Match the problem to the control.

ProblemControlWhat changes?
Feature is in the wrong placeTransform / restrained WarpPosition, scale, angle or shape
Photo shows in the wrong regionLayer maskLocal visibility
Tones should interactBlend IfVisibility/blending based on a brightness test
Correction spills onto other layersClipping relationshipWhich source/group supplies the boundary
Material is too bright or colourfulClipped tonal/colour correctionValue or colour, without solving bad alignment
Edge looks detachedMask, contact light/shadow, sharpness matchThe overlap relationship

Before touching a control: predict what you expect to see. Then make one change and compare.

I CAN / SUCCESS

What you should be able to do.

  • I can align a source to the intended facial structure.
  • I can explain and demonstrate the difference between a mask, Blend If, a blend mode and clipping.
  • I can match material scale, edge, value, colour and focus.
  • I can diagnose one mismatch, test a correction and show what changed.
  • I can apply the method to a changed source/brief while preserving editable evidence.

Success: the final image communicates the intended character; it has one clear focal area and coherent light/material; the PSD retains named masks and corrections; you can defend your choices without reading the steps. Tutorial checkmarks are practice, not grades.

Meet a changed brief, not a recipe.

Keep the same learning targets. Choose one teacher-approved direction: a subtle film-festival survivor portrait; a weathered fictional character with no exposed flesh; an environmental surface transformation; or an equivalent original brief.
