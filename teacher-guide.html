Decode the Impossible

A convincing composite is not one trick. It is a chain of editable decisions about visibility, depth, perspective, value, colour, light and shadow.

Pacing decision: five foundation classes feed a three-class assessed sprint. The sprint can be compressed to two classes only by combining testing, revision and submission with the second production class.

Your ten-minute pre-read

The expert frame

A selection identifies pixels now; a mask stores visibility so the edge can be revised later.

Layer order answers which object is in front. A mask answers which parts of that object are visible.

The out-of-bounds illusion needs two depth clues: a crossing edge above the frame and a tucked edge below it.

Edge accuracy is necessary but not sufficient. Value, colour temperature and contact shadow make sources feel photographed together.

Smart Objects and versioned PSDs are production safeguards: they preserve source quality and make decisions inspectable.

Questions students may ask

Why not erase the background?Erasing destroys pixels. A mask hides them, so clipped antennae or halos can be repaired with black and white.
Why duplicate the butterfly?The two copies occupy different depth zones. The upper copy crosses the bezel; the lower copy tucks behind it.
What makes the effect believable?No single setting. Ask whether edge, perspective, value, colour, light and shadow all tell the same spatial story.
Your through-line: Return every answer to mechanism → visible or sourced evidence → consequence. Students should hear you explain why a decision works, not only where a control is located.
Assignment map

What the product actually requires

1. Class A · Construct

Build the layered phone portal, preserve Smart Objects and create the screen mask. Finish with a Layers-panel checkpoint.

2. Class B · Break the frame

Add the masked butterfly, create one crossing edge and one tucked edge, then integrate it with tone and a contact shadow.

3. Class C · Prove and deliver

Run edge, value, lighting and peer-believability tests; revise once; export and complete the production defence.

Assessment boundary: F-P-MM12-1.1 | Decode the Impossible Composite is recommended as formative or revision-eligible. Confirm achievement by inspecting the required evidence; the website checklist and quiz never assign mastery.

Day 1

Evidence due: PSD saved in 02_working; Phone Base and Forest Portal remain separate; checkpoint screenshot includes the Layers panel.

Day 4

Out of bounds

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is How can one subject appear both inside and outside a frame? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Study three overlap diagrams and predict which parts must sit above or below the phone bezel.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

The effect needs contradictory depth evidence: most of the subject belongs to the portal, while one part occludes the bezel. Separate masks or duplicated layers control the two depth zones.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

The subject has a crossing edge and a tucked edge; the contact shadow touches the correct surface; both source and masks remain editable.

67–70 · EXIT

Explain how layer order and masking perform different jobs in the out-of-bounds effect.

Board plan

Question: How can one subject appear both inside and outside a frame?
Key language: Layer duplication or grouped masks, crossing and tucked edges, contact shadow, colour match and depth cues.
Mechanism: The effect needs contradictory depth evidence: most of the subject belongs to the portal, while one part occludes the bezel. Separate masks or duplicated layers control the two depth zones.
Evidence due: The subject has a crossing edge and a tucked edge; the contact shadow touches the correct surface; both source and masks remain editable.
