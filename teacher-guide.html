Decode the Impossible

A convincing composite is not one trick. It is a chain of editable decisions about visibility, depth, perspective, value, colour, light and shadow.

Pacing decision: three full 70-minute classes. Do not collapse the lighting build or the transfer challenge into filler time.

Your ten-minute pre-read

The expert frame

A selection identifies pixels now; a mask stores visibility so the edge can be revised later.

Layer order answers which object is in front. A mask answers which parts of that object are visible.

The out-of-bounds illusion needs two depth clues: a crossing edge above the frame and a tucked edge below it.

Edge accuracy is necessary but not sufficient. Value, colour temperature and contact shadow make sources feel photographed together.

Smart Objects and versioned PSDs are production safeguards: they preserve source quality and make decisions inspectable.

Questions students may ask

Why not erase the background?Erasing destroys pixels. A mask hides them, so clipped antennae or halos can be repaired with black and white.
Why duplicate the butterfly?The two copies occupy different depth zones. The upper copy crosses the bezel; the lower copy tucks behind it.
What makes the effect believable?No single setting. Ask whether edge, perspective, value, colour, light and shadow all tell the same spatial story.
Your through-line: Return every answer to mechanism → visible or sourced evidence → consequence. Students should hear you explain why a decision works, not only where a control is located.
Assignment map

What the product actually requires

1. Class A · Diagnose, select and construct

Gallery diagnosis and a short phone-mask preflight lead into source compatibility, Moon selection, Smart Object placement, transform/distort and the first rough composite.

2. Class B · Make the impossible believable

Isolate the chair, rebuild contact and cast shadows, create the day-for-night grade and paint local moonlight on the chair, supports, seat and ribbon.

3. Class C · Communicate, test and transfer

Add glow/rim light, complete a believability audit, add a purposeful campaign message, revise after critique and prove one unpredictable live edit.

Pathways that add real complexity

Tier 1 · Guided build

Complete the supplied Moon-as-Balloon composite with editable masks, coherent geometry, local light and shadow.

Tier 2 · Creative variation

Change the scale, placement, crop or colour world while preserving believable interaction and a clear message.

Tier 3 · Independent campaign

Transfer the workflow to new rights-safe assets and create an original surreal product advertisement or social-issue image.

Assessment boundary: F-P-MM12-1.1 | Surreal Composite Campaign is recommended as formative or revision-eligible. Confirm achievement by inspecting the required evidence; the website checklist and quiz never assign mastery.

Day 1

Diagnose, select and construct

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is Which visual problems must be solved before effects can help? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Run a six-image diagnosis sprint: students rank the most believable composite and mark the first visual tell in the weakest one.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

A composite begins with compatible viewpoint, resolution and light. Masks control visibility; transform and distort make the inserted object inherit a believable physical role.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

Concept thumbnail, compatibility gate, v01 PSD and a rough-composite screenshot with the Layers panel are saved.

67–70 · EXIT

Which source mismatch would be most expensive to repair later, and why?

Board plan

Question: Which visual problems must be solved before effects can help?
Key language: Source compatibility, mask recall, elliptical selection, Select and Mask, Smart Objects, Free Transform, Distort and contamination control.
Mechanism: A composite begins with compatible viewpoint, resolution and light. Masks control visibility; transform and distort make the inserted object inherit a believable physical role.
Evidence due: Concept thumbnail, compatibility gate, v01 PSD and a rough-composite screenshot with the Layers panel are saved.

Misconceptions to intercept

If you see:

Spending the class rebuilding the phone

Say / do:

Stop after the mask explanation and move to the new sources

If you see:

Choosing only by subject matter

Say / do:

Name one compatibility strength and one predicted problem

If you see:

Editing the downloaded source directly

Say / do:

Save the PSD before the first edit

Cold-call question: Which source mismatch would be most expensive to repair later, and why?
Listen for: a specific mechanism or design decision, evidence from the work, and a reason—not merely a description.
Day 2

Make the impossible believable

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is How must the environment respond when the Moon becomes the light source? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Toggle the supplied good and bad exemplars for five seconds. Students point to the first lighting or shadow contradiction they notice.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

The inserted Moon becomes a cause, not decoration. Nearby surfaces must receive its light; shadows must project away; local adjustments must respect form and material.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

Midpoint screenshot shows named chair isolation, contact shadow, cast shadow, global grade and at least two local moonlight layers.

67–70 · EXIT

Which layer makes the Moon feel physically connected to the chair, and what evidence proves it?

Day 3

Communicate, test and transfer

Say this to launch:
“Today’s puzzle is When does a technically convincing image become purposeful communication? Before we name tools or concepts, commit to one observation and the evidence that made you notice it.”
0–8 · HOOK

Show the final image without explanation. Reviewers write the perceived message, first focal point and least believable cue in five seconds.

8–20 · EXPERT EXPLANATION

Technical integration removes distractions; hierarchy and message determine meaning. A strong final revision targets the weakest system rather than adding more effects.

20–35 · MODEL

Demonstrate the first three actions. After each one, point to the visible result and explain why the choice matters.

35–60 · GUIDED WORK

Release students into the remaining steps. Pause once for a partner evidence check.

60–67 · CHECKPOINT

Final campaign, before/after, layered PSD, source log, critique evidence and production defence are complete; student can perform one live adjustment.

67–70 · EXIT

What changed after feedback, and why did that revision matter more than adding another effect?

Board plan

Question: When does a technically convincing image become purposeful communication?
Key language: Glow and rim light, eight-system audit, visual hierarchy, ad/PSA messaging, peer testing, targeted revision, export and live production defence.
Mechanism: Technical integration removes distractions; hierarchy and message determine meaning. A strong final revision targets the weakest system rather than adding more effects.
Evidence due: Final campaign, before/after, layered PSD, source log, critique evidence and production defence are complete; student can perform one live adjustment.
