RECORDING SCRIPT: read each slide in your own voice, one MP3 per slide.

SLIDE 01 — A client needs a message  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-01.mp3)
Ask students what an evening café invitation should make possible. A beautiful cup could read as takeaway coffee. Do not dictate the answer: show that a scene, object, headline or different concept can each serve the brief.

SLIDE 02 — Your creative decisions  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-02.mp3)
A client can require a location and a readable invitation without choosing the layout. Tell students they may propose an equivalent client. Constraints make decisions discussable; copying a model is practice, not the assessed goal.

SLIDE 03 — Audience and viewing encounter  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-03.mp3)
For CivicCycle, a new rider may need reassurance and practical information. The same audience looking across a corridor and on a phone encounters different detail. Avoid declaring that every teenager likes bright colours.

SLIDE 04 — Check the problem  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-04.mp3)
Practice feedback: the brief describes an activity that viewers have mistaken for scenery. An effect is useful only if it helps that message.

SLIDE 05 — Observation, context, interpretation  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-05.mp3)
Use the genuine historical IKEA catalogue and the contemporary identity links in Sources. State maker/date/format. “Large lettering” is an observation. The source’s account is a contextual fact. A suggested audience effect is an interpretation. Compare the different media cautiously.

SLIDE 06 — A mood board gives reasons  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-06.mp3)
A mood board is not a collage of favourite pictures. Use a small set and annotate the job of each. A linked study reference may have no production rights. The asset kit gives separate production sources.

SLIDE 07 — Three concepts  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-07.mp3)
Parallel example: a seed-library invitation could show a single seed, a growing plant sequence or a typographic exchange message. These are concepts. Three colour versions of the same seed photograph are treatments. Use this separate practice example; do not give students a client composition.

SLIDE 08 — Check the alternatives  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-08.mp3)
Practice feedback: compare the arrangement or device before the colour. Concept and treatment are different scales of decision.

SLIDE 09 — Palette roles  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-09.mp3)
The provided values are ingredients. Choose the relationship that serves the message. Complementary and analogous describe hue relationships on a specified model, not automatic readability. Compare actual text and image pairs at the intended view.

SLIDE 10 — Typography relationships  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-10.mp3)
Retrieve the distinctions from the existing typography deck and Design Lab. Text must remain editable. A strong title can fail if event details are unreadable. Font installation is unnecessary: use available school fonts and record substitutions.

SLIDE 11 — A proposal you can test  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-11.mp3)
Model with the seed-library example: “Viewers should identify an invitation to exchange seeds, then find the location.” A small viewer test can reveal a mismatch but cannot prove what all young people think. Use realistic milestones agreed with the teacher.

SLIDE 12 — Planning checkpoint  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-12.mp3)
Practice feedback: the reasons should refer to the audience, visible comparison and feasibility. Keep the alternatives. Use the existing Proposal and planning Form route after the conference.
