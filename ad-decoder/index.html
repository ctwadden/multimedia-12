0–4 minutes · A little theory first

A creative choice should solve a problem.

BRIEFWhat does the client need to communicate, and to whom?
IDEAWhat visual connection or joke will communicate it?
EXECUTIONHow will the team design, photograph, film or finish it?
Keep three things separate.
I can see… = observation.
This could suggest… = your interpretation.
The creators explain… = information from a named source.
Do not turn a guess about Photoshop into a production fact.

We are studying these brands’ communication choices, not endorsing their products or accepting their claims as facts.

4–14 minutes · Graphic design + a visual twist

McDonald’s: Follow the Arches

Cossette · Canada · 2018 · Outdoor billboards

First, look. What has the designer removed—and what still lets you understand the sign?

Open actual ads at Cossette ↗Optional campaign film ↗

Visual hierarchy is the order of emphasis in a design. Negative space is space around and between elements. Look at the large shape, the open red space and the short line of white lettering. How do they work together?

1. How does this sign communicate so quickly?

Give three short notes: one about colour, one about the cropped shape, and one about lettering or empty space. Explain what each choice helps the viewer notice or understand.

“The ______ helps the viewer ______ because ______.”

Write Question 1 in your Google Doc. Then open the story below.

REQUIRED · Why they chose it / how they made it
The brief

Cossette says the campaign was intended to direct people on the go towards nearby McDonald’s restaurants.

Where the idea came from

The team noticed the many existing signs pointing to restaurants across Canada. Its twist was to make the brand’s own arches do the pointing.

The execution

The designers enlarged and cropped sections of the logo, paired them with short directions, and used the brand’s colours. The campaign included static and digital billboards. The agency was relying on people recognising even part of the familiar identity.

Creator account: Cossette, 13 February 2018. This is a paraphrase of its campaign explanation.

Not documented here: the exact design software, font file or sequence of tool commands. We can discuss the visible crop and layout without inventing a Photoshop tutorial.

2. Why might this idea fail for a brand-new business?

Use the creator story. Explain why showing only part of an unfamiliar logo might make a new business’s sign harder to understand.

“Cossette relied on ______. For an unfamiliar business, ______ might be missing, so ______.”

Write Question 2. Move to KitKat.

14–25 minutes · Photography + a quiet visual joke

KitKat: Phone Break

VML Czechia · 2025 · Outdoor and print campaign

Look at the hands before reading the explanation. What seems normal at first—and then becomes strange?

Open actual campaign + making-of ↗

The short case film presents the campaign; it is not raw shoot footage. On VML’s page, “For Humans, By Humans” contains a separate creator mini-documentary. Neither extra video is required.

Visual substitution replaces something expected with something surprising. The viewer works out the connection. Here, the everyday pose is the setup; the unexpected object is the visual joke. You do not have to find it funny to explain how it is intended to work.

3. Explain the joke—and the product connection.

What object has been replaced? How does that replacement connect to KitKat’s idea of taking a break, rather than being a random funny picture?

“We expect to see ______, but instead see ______. This connects to the brand’s break message because ______.”

Write Question 3. Then open the creator story.

REQUIRED · Where the idea came from / what happened on the shoot
The brief and the spark

VML says the brief was to make the familiar break message relevant to a highly connected world. In its creator film, a team member describes noticing phone use on the walk to work. VML also points out the similar oblong shapes of phones and KitKat bars.

They chose an ordinary-looking scene on purpose

In the team’s award-entry account, real locations, natural lighting and subtle styling were chosen to make the situations recognisable. The people’s poses imitate phone use; the chocolate is the unexpected substitution.

Photography plus finishing—not a guessed filter

The published credits name a photographer and credit B612 Studio for retouching. That confirms a photographic production with retouching, but does not reveal every edit or the software used.

Creator sources: VML campaign page and creator film; the team’s One Show entry, “Execution”; published production credits. Campaign: 2025; award-entry page: 2026.

Important: a photograph can look candid even when carefully staged. “Natural lighting” does not mean “no editing.” The campaign’s commercial message is not evidence that eating chocolate improves wellbeing.

4. Why did the ordinary setting matter?

Name one production choice confirmed by the sources—such as location, lighting, styling or poses. Explain how it helps the visual joke work.

“The team’s account confirms ______. This helps because ______.”

Write Question 4. No need to guess a Photoshop tool.

25–36 minutes · Absurd humour + practical production

Old Spice: The Man Your Man Could Smell Like

Wieden+Kennedy · 2010 · Short video advertisement

Watch the approximately 30-second advertisement. First notice the surprise. On a second viewing, track how often the product or its name returns.

A shower. A boat. A horse.

Original brand video · YouTube

Open on YouTube ↗

Content: a shirtless adult in a towel; a deliberately exaggerated, gendered sales pitch from 2010. We are examining the choices, not treating its stereotypes as rules about people. Use captions where available.

Video blocked or not suitable for you? Read this description.

The presenter talks directly to the viewer while promoting body wash. The scene changes from a shower to a boat, and finishes with him sitting on a horse. His delivery stays confident while the situation becomes increasingly unlikely. The product is shown and named during the pitch.

This description is enough to complete Questions 5–6 alongside the creator account below. State that you used the description rather than claiming to have watched.

Incongruity is a mismatch between what you expect and what happens. Exaggeration pushes an idea beyond ordinary reality. Our interpretation: the calm delivery and increasingly unlikely situations create the contrast that drives this ad’s humour.

Brand connection test: after the surprise, do you remember what was being advertised?

5. Is the humour doing a job?

Identify one surprising moment. Explain how the advertisement connects the entertainment back to body wash—or why you think the joke could distract from it.

“The moment ______ is unexpected because ______. The product stays connected / risks being forgotten because ______.”

Write Question 5. Then read how the team made it.

REQUIRED · The writing decision / the production solution
The audience decision

Wieden+Kennedy says it wanted a conversation between couples, including people buying body wash for someone else. It placed the campaign where couples might watch together. That was this campaign’s strategy, not a rule about who buys products today.

They worked on the words before solving the images

In a creator interview, the writers explain that they first developed the dialogue. Their creative directors then suggested showing the situations the presenter was describing.

How the impossible scene changes were built

The team describes a continuous take using physical sets and a device that moved the actor sideways from the boat setup onto a real horse. Director Tom Kuntz favoured practical tricks because their imperfections could add comic charm. Some digital effects were also used; “one take” does not mean “no post-production.”

Strategy: Wieden+Kennedy’s own case study. Writing and production: creator interviews in Muse by Clios, “Behind the Towel” (2020), and Creative Review (2021). The full interview links are in the teacher-source section; their optional long-form material contains adult language.

Practical effect: made physically during the shoot. Digital effect: created or altered using computer-based processes. Neither term automatically tells you whether the advertising message is truthful.

6. How did the production choice support the idea?

Name one confirmed writing or production decision from the creator account. Explain how it supports surprise, humour or the product message. Keep the fact separate from your interpretation.

“The creators explain that they ______. I think this supports ______ because ______.”

Write Question 6. No technical procedure or new design is required.
