3–10 minutes / First case / Questions 1–2

McDonald’s — Follow the Arches

Cossette · Canada · 2018 · Outdoor campaign

Look before opening the story. What has been removed? What still makes the sign understandable?

Visual hierarchy is the order of emphasis. Negative space is the space around or between elements. Here, test how a familiar shape, a restricted palette and very few words work together.

1. How does this sign communicate so quickly?

Give three short notes: one about colour, one about the cropped shape, and one about lettering or empty space. Explain what each helps the viewer notice or understand.

“The _____ helps the viewer _____ because _____.”

Write Q1 in your Set A Doc, then open the story.

REQUIRED / What Cossette chose and why

Brief and idea: Cossette describes noticing existing signs directing people to nearby McDonald’s restaurants. The team turned sections of the Golden Arches into directional graphics.

Execution: The agency used cropped logo fragments, established brand colours and short instructions rather than a full logo plus a separate arrow. This made recognition part of the design idea.

Source limit: the agency confirms the cropping concept and visual treatment, not a particular Photoshop tool. Whether a viewer recognises the fragment is something to question, not assume.

Source: Cossette’s account of Follow the Arches.

2. Why might this fail for a brand-new business?

Use the Cossette source story. Explain why showing only part of an unfamiliar logo might make a new business’s sign harder to understand.

“Cossette relied on _____. For a new business, _____ might be missing, so _____.”

Write Q2. Move on by minute 10.

10–17 minutes / Second case / Questions 3–4

KitKat — Phone Break

VML · 2025 · Photographic campaign

Incongruity is a mismatch between what you expect and what appears. Visual substitution replaces one familiar object with another. A useful test: does the surprise connect to the brand’s message?

3. Explain the joke and the product connection.

What object has been replaced? How does that replacement connect to KitKat’s idea of taking a break, rather than being a random funny picture?

“We expect _____, but see _____. This connects to the break message because _____.”

Write Q3 before opening the story.

REQUIRED / Why the ordinary scene mattered

Idea: VML connects the phone-shaped chocolate bar with the brand’s established break message, substituting a KitKat for a device in recognisable everyday situations.

Production: The team’s award-entry account describes real environments, natural lighting, subtle styling and carefully staged gestures. Those choices were intended to make the situations feel familiar while the replacement object created the twist.

These sources document staging and visual treatment. They do not provide a Photoshop layer stack or confirm the exact tool used for each part.

Sources: VML’s campaign case study and creator-submitted production account at The One Club. Campaign: 2025; the cited award entry is from 2026.

4. Why did the ordinary setting matter?

Name one production choice confirmed in the source story, such as location, lighting, styling or poses. Explain how it helps the visual joke work.

“The team’s account confirms _____. I think this helps because _____.”

Write Q4. Move on by minute 17.

17–25 minutes / Third case / Questions 5–6

Old Spice — The Man Your Man Could Smell Like

Wieden+Kennedy · 2010 · Approximately 30-second advertisement

A shower. A boat. A horse.

Watch once. Notice the surprise and the product connection.

Open on YouTube ↗

Content: a shirtless adult in a towel and an exaggerated, gendered sales pitch from 2010. Study its choices critically; they are not rules about people. The written route below is equally acceptable.

Video blocked or not suitable for you? Use this description.

A presenter addresses the viewer while promoting body wash. The setting changes from a shower to a boat, and ends with him on a horse. His delivery stays confident as the situations become increasingly unlikely. The product is shown and named. State in your answer that you used this description.

Exaggeration pushes beyond ordinary reality. Our interpretation: calm delivery contrasted with increasingly unlikely situations drives the humour. Test whether the entertainment keeps the product in view.

5. Is the humour doing a job?

Identify one surprising moment. Explain how the ad connects the entertainment back to body wash, or why the joke could distract from it.

“_____ is unexpected because _____. The product stays connected / risks being forgotten because _____.”

Write Q5, then read the production account.

REQUIRED / Writing first, then solving the spectacle

Audience: Wieden+Kennedy says its strategy considered couples, including people buying body wash for someone else.

Writing: In interviews, the writers explain that they developed the dialogue before fully resolving the visuals. Creative directors suggested showing the situations the presenter described.

Production: The creators describe physical sets and a device that transferred the performer from the boat setup onto a real horse. Director Tom Kuntz valued the comic charm of practical tricks. Digital effects were also involved; do not call it “no post-production”.

Sources: Wieden+Kennedy’s case study; creator interviews in Muse by Clios, “Behind the Towel” (2020). The long interview is linked in the teacher notes because it contains adult language.

6. How did the production choice support the idea?

Name one confirmed writing or production decision from the creator interview summary. Explain how it supports surprise, humour or the product message.

“The creators explain that they _____. I think this supports _____ because _____.”

3–10 minutes / First case / Questions 1–2

IKEA — Cook This Page

Leo Burnett Toronto · Canada · 2017 · Printed promotional recipe posters

Look first. How is this different from an advertisement that only shows a finished meal?

Hierarchy helps distinguish a title, directions and details. Usable design gives clues about what to do next. In this example, investigate how the outlines suggest what to place where.

1. How does the layout help someone take action?

Give three short notes: one about the ingredient shapes, one about lettering, and one about placement or empty space. Explain what each helps the user notice or do.

“The _____ helps the user _____ because _____.”

Write Q1 in your Set B Doc, then open the story.

REQUIRED / The material was part of the idea

Brief: Leo Burnett says IKEA wanted new recipes to feel less intimidating and kitchen creativity to feel simple.

Execution: The agency made posters using food-safe ink on parchment cooking paper. Ingredient spaces were measured, and the recipes included IKEA food products. The printed outlines helped people arrange the ingredients; the same sheet was designed to be rolled and used for cooking.

Why this matters: The campaign offered a usable activity, not just a picture. Its layout, material and measurements needed to work together.

Sources: Leo Burnett Design’s production account and D&AD’s campaign archive.

Design analysis only. Do not put ordinary paper or printer ink in an oven. No cooking or printing this product today.

2. Why did the material and measurements matter?

Use the Leo Burnett source story. Name one confirmed production choice and explain why a picture of a delicious meal would not do the same job.

“The team used _____. This lets people _____, whereas a normal food photo _____.”

10–17 minutes / Second case / Questions 3–4

Specsavers — Parking Fail

Specsavers / Grand Visual / MG OMD · 2024 stunt · Agency production account published 2025

Context is the surrounding situation. Incongruity is a mismatch with expectations. Here, the street location and brand name help frame the joke, rather than a long written explanation.

3. Explain the joke and the brand connection.

What seems to have gone wrong? Explain how a visible detail connects the staged situation to an eye-care brand. You may also question whether the joke works.

“We expect _____, but see _____. The brand connection is _____ because _____.”

Write Q3, then read how it was staged.

REQUIRED / An apparent accident, built on purpose

Brief: Grand Visual says the team wanted people to talk about the “Should’ve gone to Specsavers” brand.

Execution: It describes a fake branded van staged in Edinburgh and Liverpool. Bollards were built and signage added. Details included working hazard lights and a parking ticket on the windscreen.

Reason: The agency says making the scene look natural and believable was intended to encourage social and press sharing. This is a documented physical setup—not proof of a real accident and not a reason to invent a Photoshop method.

Source: Grand Visual’s own production explanation.

Discuss the staged brand joke, not real people with sight loss. The advertisement does not establish that an eye condition makes someone careless.

4. Why make a staged scene look believable?

Name one detail Grand Visual confirms it deliberately arranged. Explain how the choice could attract attention or encourage people to share the scene.

“Grand Visual confirms _____. I think this could _____ because _____.”

Write Q4. Move on by minute 17.

17–25 minutes / Third case / Questions 5–6

Honda — Cog

Wieden+Kennedy · 2003 · Two-minute film

Car parts become a chain reaction.

Watch the original ad hosted through the agency’s page. Notice what happens before the finished car appears.

Open agency film page ↗
Video blocked? Read this description instead.

Parts from a disassembled Honda Accord roll, tilt and collide in sequence. Each movement triggers the next; the camera follows the chain until the finished car becomes the final reveal. The arrangement appears carefully coordinated. Use this description plus the production account to answer, and state that you did not watch the film.

Visual demonstration shows an idea through an action. Our interpretation: the carefully coordinated parts can suggest precision. An advertising demonstration is not an independent test of how reliable the car is.

5. What is the chain reaction trying to communicate?

Identify one moment and explain what it could suggest about the car or brand. Is a creative demonstration the same as evidence that a car is reliable? Briefly explain.

“The moment _____ could suggest _____. It does / does not prove reliability because _____.”

Write Q5, then read the production account.

REQUIRED / Planning and editing behind the apparent ease

What Honda’s retrospective reports: The sequence used parts from a disassembled Accord. Preparing the production involved months of approval, setup and testing, with engineers, sculptors and artists advising.

How it was finished: Honda’s published account describes two takes joined together, filmed over five days. The appearance of one flowing event therefore does not establish that it was one unedited take.

Keep the attribution: This is Honda’s 2020 published retrospective, including marketing commentary—not an interview we conducted with the original team. Explain separately what you think these decisions do for the message.

Source: Honda’s Cog retrospective, especially “Creatively, why is the ‘Cog’ campaign so special?” The page also includes later audience remakes; those are not the original ad used here.

6. How did planning and editing support the idea?

Name one production detail reported in Honda’s retrospective. Explain how it supports the appearance of a continuous chain reaction. Do not claim it was one unedited take.

“Honda’s account reports _____. I think that helps _____ because _____.”

Write Q6. At minute 25, go to the exchange instructions.
