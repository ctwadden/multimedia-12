Industry connection

Key art sells a film before anyone sees it.

Studios commission “key art”: the single image used on posters, streaming tiles and billboards. Designers build it in Photoshop from photographs, and they light it the way a cinematographer lights a set. Every highlight is motivated by a source the audience can believe. S1

Posters follow conventions viewers read without thinking: a title with one strong typeface, a tagline, and a condensed billing block of credits at the bottom. S2 Today most key art also ships as a motion poster.

Knowledge first

How light works, and how Photoshop fakes it.

Light obeys rules

  • Direction: light travels in straight lines from its source. Highlights face the source; shadows point away.
  • Fall-off: light gets dimmer with distance, so it is brightest at the source and fades smoothly.
  • Colour temperature: fire, lamps and sunsets are warm; moonlight, shade and screens are cool.
  • Edges catch light: the edge of a subject turned towards a source gets a rim highlight.
  • Air shows light: fog, dust and smoke make beams and glow visible.

Design principles in a poster

Emphasis and focal point: the brightest, highest-contrast area is where the eye goes first, so put the story there. Value contrast: a dark world makes the light read as bright. Colour harmony: warm light against cool shadow (complementary contrast) separates subject and background. Hierarchy in type: title, then tagline, then credits.

The Photoshop method

Never paint light directly on the photo. Use adjustment layers (Curves) with masks: an inverted mask hides the adjustment everywhere, and a soft white brush paints it back only where light lands. Blend modes add colour (Soft Light) and glow (Screen, Color Dodge). Because it stays on layers, you can move or animate the light later.

Read the brief and plan the light

IN SHORTChoose one real light source; sketch it, arrow it, circle what it hits.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: This guide · a sketch page or LastLight_plan.txt
SELECT: One light source and where it sits
TOOLS / ROUTE: Thumbnail · arrow for light direction
  1. Read the film brief in Start. Invent your film’s title and a one-line story (or use LAST LIGHT).
  2. Choose your background plate: one of your own Shot on Purpose photos, or a plate from the starter pack.

03

Set the mood: darken the whole world

IN SHORTCurves darken; cool Soft Light fill; group as MOOD.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: Everything (the adjustment sits at the top)
TOOLS / ROUTE: Layer › New Adjustment Layer › Curves · Solid Color
  1. Click the top layer, then add a Curves adjustment layer. Name it MOOD darken.
  2. Drag the middle of the curve down until the whole scene looks like late dusk. The brightest area should still be clearly lighter than the rest.
  3. Add a Solid Color fill layer (a dark blue such as #1E3050). Set its blend mode to Soft Light and opacity to about 40 %.
  4. Compare before and after by clicking the eye icons. The scene should feel quiet and cold before any light returns.
  5. Group the two layers (Ctrl/Cmd+G) and name the group MOOD.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Curves: An adjustment that remaps brightness; dragging the middle changes midtones
Soft Light: A blend mode that tints and adds gentle contrast
WHYLight only reads as bright against darkness. Darkening first gives your light somewhere to go.
CHECKThe scene is clearly darker and cooler, the planned light area is still the lightest part, and the MOOD group can be switched off.
FIXEverything muddy? Drag the top point of the curve back up slightly. Too blue? Lower the fill opacity.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTA night-time bus-stop poster used the same move: darken everything, then let the shelter light pull you in.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Try a warm-dark version (brown fill) and a cold-dark version (blue fill). Which fits your story?

04

Paint the light back in through a mask

IN SHORTCurves up; invert mask; soft white brush at low opacity; build light out from the source.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The area your light source should brighten
TOOLS / ROUTE: Curves · Ctrl/Cmd+I on the mask · Brush (B), soft, 10–20 % opacity
  1. Add a new Curves adjustment above MOOD and name it LIGHT paint. Drag the middle of the curve up strongly, so everything is too bright.
  2. Click its white mask thumbnail and press Ctrl/Cmd+I to invert it to black. The brightening disappears.
  3. Choose the Brush (B): soft round, white, Opacity 10–20 %, Flow 100 %.
  4. Start at your light source and paint outward in short strokes, building up light where it is strongest and letting it fade with distance.
  5. Paint the light onto the surfaces you circled in your plan: the ground in front of the source, the side of the figure facing it.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Invert a mask: Swap black and white, so the adjustment is hidden everywhere until you paint it in
Fall-off: Light getting dimmer the further it travels from its source
WHYAn inverted mask hides the adjustment everywhere, so you choose exactly where light lands. Low opacity lets you build it gradually, like real light falling off.
CHECKThe light is brightest at its source, falls off smoothly, and lands only on the surfaces the plan says it should.
FIXHard edges? Your brush hardness is above 0 %. Too strong? Paint black at low opacity to pull it back, or lower the layer opacity.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTBenny Productions paints highlights on a portrait the same way: one Curves layer, black mask, soft white brush.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Add a second LIGHT paint layer just for the brightest core, with a smaller brush.
Method basis: A1 · A2 · V1 · V2. Values and sequence are authored for this project.
05

Read and shape the mask

IN SHORTView the mask; smooth it; feather; adjust density; save.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The LIGHT paint mask
TOOLS / ROUTE: Alt/Option-click the mask · Properties › Feather and Density
  1. Alt/Option-click the LIGHT paint mask thumbnail to see the mask on its own.
  2. Check the shape: it should be a soft pool that is brightest at the source and fades out, with no stripes or blotches.
  3. Fix stripes by painting over them with a large soft brush at low opacity. Alt/Option-click again to return to the image.
  4. Open the Properties panel with the mask selected. Raise Feather (for example 20–40 px) to soften the whole pool.
  5. Lower Density if the light looks painted on everywhere. Save your file.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Feather: Softens a mask’s edges without repainting
Density: How strongly a mask hides; lower density lets some of the effect through everywhere
WHYThe mask is the light. Learning to read it tells you exactly why the image looks the way it does.
CHECKA smooth mask with no stripes, brightest at the source, and a result that looks like light rather than paint.
FIXCan’t get back to the image? Alt/Option-click the mask thumbnail again. Feather greyed out? Click the mask thumbnail, not the adjustment icon.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTA student found a stripe in the mask across the sky that they had never noticed in the image. It was a single hard brush stroke.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Duplicate the mask to a new Curves darken layer and invert it, so the areas furthest from the light fall into shadow.
Method basis: A2. Values and sequence are authored for this project.

06

Give the light a colour temperature

IN SHORTWarm Solid Color on Soft Light; copy the light mask; balance opacity.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The lit area (warm) and the shadows (cool)
TOOLS / ROUTE: Solid Color fill · Soft Light · paste the light mask
  1. Add a Solid Color fill layer in a warm light colour (for example #FFA046). Set it to Soft Light and name it LIGHT colour.
  2. Alt/Option-drag the LIGHT paint mask onto LIGHT colour to copy it, so the colour lands only where the light does.
  3. Lower its opacity until the light looks warm but not orange (usually 50–80 %).
  4. Check the shadows: they should stay cool from the MOOD fill. Warm light against cool shadow is the contrast that feels cinematic.
  5. Write the hex codes of your light colour and shadow colour in your plan.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Colour temperature: How warm (orange) or cool (blue) light looks
Complementary contrast: Opposite colours side by side, such as orange light and blue shadow
WHYColour temperature tells the viewer what the light is: warm for sunset, fire and lamps; cool for moonlight, screens and shade. Warm against cool also separates the subject from the background.
CHECKThe lit area is warm, the shadows stay cool, and the colour follows the light mask exactly.
FIXColour everywhere? The mask was not copied: Alt/Option-drag the mask thumbnail. Looks orange? Lower opacity or choose a paler colour.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTMovie posters use warm skin against teal backgrounds so often that it has a nickname: “orange and teal”.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Try a cool light source instead (a phone or moon) with warm shadows. How does the story change?

07

Make the figure belong: match it and add a rim light

IN SHORTClip a Curves to darken; clipped RIM layer on Color Dodge; paint the light-facing edge only.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The FIGURE layer and the edge that faces the light
TOOLS / ROUTE: Clipped Curves · new layer, Color Dodge or Screen · soft brush
  1. Add a Curves adjustment directly above FIGURE and Alt/Option-click between them to clip it. Darken the figure until it matches the scene’s darkness.
  2. Add a new empty layer, clip it to FIGURE and set it to Color Dodge (or Screen). Name it RIM.
  3. Pick your light colour. With a small soft brush at 20–30 % opacity, paint a thin line of light along the edge of the figure that faces the source only.
  4. Make the rim strongest where the edge turns most directly towards the light (forehead, nose, shoulder), and let it fade away round the back.
  5. Zoom out and check every highlight on the figure could come from your one source.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Rim light: A thin highlight on the edge of a subject, from light behind or beside it
Clipping mask: Makes a layer affect only the layer directly below it
WHYA rim light separates the figure from the background and proves which way the light comes from. Light on the wrong side is the first thing viewers notice as fake.
CHECKThe figure is as dark as its surroundings, and a thin warm rim appears only on the side facing the source.
FIXRim spilling past the edge? It must be clipped: Alt/Option-click between RIM and FIGURE. Glowing like neon? Lower RIM opacity or use Screen instead of Color Dodge.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTOn a real film set a “kicker” light behind the actor does this job. You are painting that kicker.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Add a soft cast shadow from the figure, pointing away from the light: duplicate the mask shape, fill it black, Distort it along the ground, blur it, set Multiply.
Method basis: A3 · V2 · V4. Values and sequence are authored for this project.
08

Fill the air: glow, rays and haze

IN SHORTRadial glow, zoom-blurred rays and clouds haze, all on Screen; group ATMOSPHERE.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The air around the light source
TOOLS / ROUTE: Radial gradient on Screen · Filter › Blur › Radial Blur (Zoom) · Filter › Render › Clouds
  1. Glow: new layer set to Screen. With the Gradient tool (G), radial, your light colour to transparent, drag outward from the source.
  2. Rays: new layer. Paint a few rough white strokes spreading from the source, then choose Filter › Blur › Radial Blur, Zoom, Amount 80–100, and drag the blur centre onto the source. Set to Screen at 30–50 %.
  3. Haze: new layer, press D, then Filter › Render › Clouds. Set it to Screen at 15–25 % and mask it off the figure’s face.
  4. Keep all three between the figure and the light, never brighter than the source itself.
  5. Group them as ATMOSPHERE. Toggle the group to check it helps and does not wash out the image.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Volumetric light: Light made visible by particles in the air
Screen: A blend mode that only lightens; black disappears
WHYLight is only visible in the air when something (fog, dust, smoke) catches it. Adding it makes the light feel physical and pulls the eye to the source.
CHECKA glow around the source, rays that spread from it, haze that thickens with distance, and the figure still readable.
FIXRays point the wrong way? Undo the Radial Blur and set its centre on the source. Everything grey? Lower the haze opacity or mask it more.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTThe demo’s fog already caught the light, so only a small glow and faint rays were needed.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Add dust particles: a scatter brush with a few tiny white dots on Screen, mostly in the beam.
Method basis: A3 · V3. Values and sequence are authored for this project.

11

Put a glowing object in the frame and let it light the subject

IN SHORTStudy the plate; place the object; blur glow and halo; paint cast light nearest first; tint and darken the far side.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd · starter sparkler.jpg, flashlight.jpg or a drawn light
SELECT: A light source in the character’s hand or near their face
TOOLS / ROUTE: Place · Screen/Lighten · Outer Glow · Curves + Color Balance through masks · Color Dodge
  1. Study the sparkler plate: the hand is brightest next to the flame, warm-orange, and falls off quickly. Your subject must behave the same way.
  2. Place your glowing object (a sparkler, lantern, torch or phone) in the character’s hand or near the face. Set bright elements shot on black to Screen so the black disappears.
  3. Make the source glow: duplicate it, Gaussian Blur 20–40 px, set to Screen or Linear Dodge (Add); add a larger, softer copy for the halo.
  4. Cast its light: a new Curves layer, brightened, with a black mask. Paint the light onto the nearest surfaces first (fingers, chin, underside of the nose, the front of the coat), strongest closest to the source.
  5. Tint that light with a clipped Color Balance or a Color Dodge layer in the source’s colour, and darken the side of the face away from the object.
Watch the technique, then apply it to your poster:
  • How to create a glow effect (Adobe Photoshop / Benny): building the glow and halo
  • How to glow anything (Webflippy): making an object glow and spill light onto nearby surfaces
  • Stylish highlights (Benny Productions): coloured highlight painting
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Cast light: Light from one object landing on another
Linear Dodge (Add): A blend mode that adds brightness, useful for glows
WHYA glowing object is the most convincing motivated light because the viewer sees its source. If nearby skin and cloth do not pick up its colour and fall-off, the composite looks pasted.
CHECKThe object glows with a soft halo, and the surfaces closest to it are brighter and tinted in its colour, falling off quickly with distance.
FIXGlow looks like a sticker? The subject is not receiving the light: paint more cast light on the nearest surfaces. Everything orange? Keep the cast light to the side facing the object.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTThe Spider-Man poster uses the same idea with the glowing logo and city lights. Every glow in the frame lights something next to it.

Blend the edges with light wrap, add particles and grade

IN SHORTLight wrap on facing edges; embers in the beam with depth; grade; compare with the pro final.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The subject’s edges and the air around the light
TOOLS / ROUTE: Stamp visible · Gaussian Blur · clip · Screen · particle brush · Gradient Map
  1. Light wrap: stamp the background (hide the subject, Ctrl/Cmd+Alt/Option+Shift+E), blur it 20–30 px, clip it to the subject and set it to Screen. Mask it so it only shows on the edges facing the bright background.
  2. Particles: on a new layer, use a scatter brush (Size Jitter and Scattering on) to paint embers or dust in the beam of the glowing object. Set to Color Dodge; blur a copy for depth.
  3. Depth: blur the particles nearest the camera more than the ones near the subject.
  4. Grade: add a Gradient Map (dark teal to warm cream) on Soft Light at 20–30 %, then a Curves vignette. Check the glowing object is still the brightest point.
  5. Export the finished key art. Compare side by side with the Nour Art or Mir Rom final: name one thing yours does as well and one thing to improve.

  • High-end advertising posters (Nour Art): finishing passes: light wrap, particles, grade
  • Cinematic movie poster (Mir Rom): atmosphere and final grade
  • Master shadows and lighting in compositing (PiXimperfect): matching light and colour so the subject belongs
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Light wrap: Background light spilling around a subject’s edges
Particles: Small bright specks such as embers or dust that show light in the air
WHYLight wrap is how compositors make a subject sit inside a bright scene instead of on top of it. Particles show the air the light travels through. The grade ties it together.
CHECKEdges that pick up the background light, particles concentrated in the light beam with depth, and a unified grade with the glowing object brightest.
FIXLight wrap glows all round the subject? Mask it to the edges that face bright areas only. Particles look flat? Vary size and blur, and keep most of them in the light.

TOOLS / ROUTE: Type tool (T) · Character panel · Align
  1. Title: one strong typeface, large, near the top or bottom third. Pick a light colour sampled from your light (Eyedropper).
  2. Tagline: a short hook in a clean sans serif, much smaller than the title.
  3. Billing block: at the bottom, set in a condensed typeface, all capitals, two or three lines, small. Use your own names and roles.
  4. Date or call to action: “COMING SOON” or a date, just above or below the billing block.
  5. Align everything to one centre line. Keep type out of the light source and off the figure’s face.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Tagline: A short line that sells the story
Billing block: The condensed credit lines at the bottom of a film poster
Tracking: The overall spacing between letters
WHYPoster type follows a hierarchy readers expect: title first, tagline second, credits last. Type in the light’s colour makes it feel part of the same world.
CHECKFour type elements in clear order of size, aligned, in two typefaces at most, and none covering the light or the face.
FIXTitle hard to read? Add a subtle dark Curves behind it, masked to the title area. Billing block too wide? Reduce tracking or font size, not the letter width.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTThe demo’s title is set in Didot, tagline and date in Futura, and billing block in Arial Narrow. That is three fonts, but the billing block is expected to be condensed.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Add a small studio logo you design yourself, placed in the billing block.

Practical check: move the light

IN SHORTExport A; read the note; change every light-dependent layer; check; export B; explain.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd · the director’s note
SELECT: Every layer that depends on the light
TOOLS / ROUTE: Masks · RIM · ATMOSPHERE · Radial Blur centre
  1. Export your finished poster as LastLight_YourName_A.jpg. This is your “before”.
  2. Read the director’s note: “Move the light source to the other side of the frame.” (Your teacher may give a different note: a different height, time of day or colour.)
  3. Work out on your own which layers must change. Make the changes: repaint or flip the light masks, move the glow and rays, and repaint the rim on the other edge.
  4. Check every highlight, rim, ray and shadow now agrees with the new source position.
  5. Export LastLight_YourName_B.jpg. In three sentences explain which layers you changed, why, and one thing that was harder than expected.

Independent evidence: this step is the main achievement evidence for Last Light. Your teacher may watch you make one change live.

STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Consistency: Every lit surface agreeing on one light direction
WHYIf you built the light with adjustment layers and masks, moving it is a set of targeted edits, not a restart. This shows you understand how the light was made.
CHECKPoster B has a single believable source in its new position, with rim, rays, glow and colour all consistent. Your explanation names the layers you changed.
FIXStuck? Turn layers off one at a time to see which ones hold the old light direction. (Independent step: the Coach can explain terms but not decide your edits.)
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTWorked example on a different image: a student moved a lamp from left to right in a bedroom scene and had to repaint the face highlight, the wall glow and the cast shadow.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Change the time of day as well as the side: turn dusk into dawn with a cooler, paler light.

15

Make the light move: a 5-second teaser

IN SHORTVideo Timeline; opacity keyframes for light, atmosphere and title; render MP4; hand in.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: LIGHT paint, ATMOSPHERE and RIM
TOOLS / ROUTE: Window › Timeline · Create Video Timeline · Opacity keyframes · Render Video
  1. Choose Window › Timeline and click Create Video Timeline. Set the length to 5 seconds (drag the end of the work area).
  2. Open the LIGHT paint layer’s track, click the stopwatch next to Opacity, and set keyframes: 0 % at 0 s, 100 % at 2 s.
  3. Add a flicker or breathing light: a few quick opacity keyframes on ATMOSPHERE (for example 60 %, 90 %, 70 %) between 2 s and 4 s.
  4. Fade the title in from 0 % to 100 % between 3 s and 4 s. Hold the full poster to 5 s.
  5. Choose File › Export › Render Video, H.264, 1080 × 1620, and save LastLight_YourName_teaser.mp4. Hand in A, B, the teaser, the PSD and your explanation.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Keyframe: A saved value at a moment in time; the software fills in the frames between
Motion poster: A poster with subtle movement, made for screens
WHYFilm posters now run as motion posters on social media and in cinemas. Because your light is on its own layers, you can animate it without redrawing anything.
CHECKA 5-second MP4 where the light fades up, the atmosphere flickers, and the title appears, plus all hand-in files.
FIXNo Timeline panel? Choose Window › Timeline. Render too slow? Use 720 × 1080. Keyframes not saving? Click the stopwatch first.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTA student’s “The Late Shift” teaser flickered the fridge light twice before the title appeared. It lasted four seconds and was very effective.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Add a slow push-in: keyframe the key art’s Transform from 100 % to 104 % over 5 seconds.
