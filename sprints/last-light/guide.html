Industry connection

Key art sells a film before anyone sees it.

Studios commission “key art”: the single image used on posters, streaming tiles and billboards. Designers build it in Photoshop from photographs, and they light it the way a cinematographer lights a set. Every highlight is motivated by a source the audience can believe. S1

Posters follow conventions viewers read without thinking: a title with one strong typeface, a tagline, and a condensed billing block of credits at the bottom. S2 Today most key art also ships as a motion poster.

Watch (technique)

  • Stylish highlights and realistic highlights (Benny Productions): the core painting-light method.
  • Cinematic movie poster (Mir Rom): mood, grade and atmosphere.
  • For teacher prep or extension: Nour Art’s Spider-Man manipulation and Hazy Graphix’s Avengers remake. These are great techniques, but they use existing characters, so your poster must be original.
Knowledge first

How light works, and how Photoshop fakes it.

Light obeys rules

  • Direction: light travels in straight lines from its source. Highlights face the source; shadows point away.
  • Fall-off: light gets dimmer with distance, so it is brightest at the source and fades smoothly.
  • Colour: the demo uses a stylised green energy light, teal shadows and later warm accents. Physical colour temperature and a fictional green effect are different ideas.
  • Edges catch light: the edge of a subject turned towards a source gets a rim highlight.
  • Air shows light: fog, dust and smoke make beams and glow visible.

Design principles in a poster

Emphasis and focal point: the brightest, highest-contrast area is where the eye goes first, so put the story there. Value contrast: a dark world makes the light read as bright. Colour harmony: controlled green/teal and later warm accents distinguish illuminated surfaces from the night scene. Hierarchy in type: title, then tagline, then credits.

The Photoshop method

Never paint light directly on the photo. Use adjustment layers (Curves) with masks: an inverted mask hides the adjustment everywhere, and a soft white brush paints it back only where light lands. Blend modes add colour (Soft Light) and glow (Screen, Color Dodge). Because it stays on layers, you can move or animate the light later.

Paint the light back in through a mask

IN SHORTCurves up; invert mask; soft white brush at low opacity; build light out from the source.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The area your light source should brighten
TOOLS / ROUTE: Curves · Ctrl/Cmd+I on the mask · Brush (B), soft, 10–20 % opacity
  1. Add a Curves adjustment above MOOD named LIGHT paint. Set RGB points 0→0, 90→175 and 255→255. The original 90→175 point has been checked on screen.
  2. Click its white mask thumbnail and press Ctrl/Cmd+I to invert it to black. The brightening disappears.

  4. Start at your light source and paint outward in short strokes, building up light where it is strongest and letting it fade with distance.
  5. Paint the light onto the surfaces you circled in your plan: the ground in front of the source, the side of the figure facing it.

Read and shape the mask

IN SHORTView the mask; smooth it; feather; keep Density 100 %; adjust layer opacity; save.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: LastLight_YourName.psd
SELECT: The LIGHT paint mask
TOOLS / ROUTE: Channels › mask visibility / RGB visibility · Properties › Feather and Density
  1. Click LIGHT paint’s mask. Open Channels, turn the LIGHT paint Mask eye on, then turn the RGB eye off to read the mask alone.
  2. Check the shape: it should be a soft pool that is brightest at the source and fades out, with no stripes or blotches.
  3. Smooth stripes with a large soft brush at low opacity. Restore colour: turn the mask eye off, click RGB, then return to Layers.
  4. With the mask selected, open Properties and set Feather to 30 px. Keep Density at 100%.
  5. Keep mask Density at 100 %. Lower the LIGHT paint layer opacity to reduce the adjustment strength. Lower Density reveals the adjustment across hidden areas and can destroy the intended isolation. Save your file.

Action by action: real Photoshop screenshots (orange boxes show where to look), and the result

1With LIGHT paint’s mask selected, open Channels (1). Turn LIGHT paint Mask visibility on (2), then turn RGB visibility off (3). Read white as light revealed and black as light hidden.

2To restore the image, turn the mask eye off, click the RGB channel row, and return to Layers. The colour poster should return.

3Click the mask thumbnail and open Properties. Keep Density at 100% (1). Enter Feather 30 px (2) to soften transitions without revealing the adjustment everywhere.

STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Feather: Softens a mask’s edges without repainting
Density: How strongly a mask hides; lower density lets some of the effect through everywhere
WHYThe mask is the light. Learning to read it tells you exactly why the image looks the way it does.
CHECKA smooth mask with no stripes, brightest at the source, and a result that looks like light rather than paint.
FIXRed overlay instead of greyscale? RGB is still visible: turn its eye off. To restore colour, hide the mask channel and click RGB. Feather unavailable? Select the mask thumbnail.

STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Duplicate the mask to a new Curves darken layer and invert it, so the areas furthest from the light fall into shadow.
