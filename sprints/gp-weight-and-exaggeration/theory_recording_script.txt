RECORDING SCRIPT: read each slide in your own voice, one MP3 per slide.

SLIDE 01 — Nothing on screen weighs anything  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-01.mp3)
Nothing on a screen weighs anything. The audience decides how heavy a thing is, and what it is made of, from how it moves. There are four ideas in this set of lessons. Timing shows weight. Volume shows material. And exaggeration and staging make sure the audience can read it.

SLIDE 02 — Heavy is fast to fall and quick to stop  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-02.mp3)
A heavy object speeds up as it falls, so its drawings get further and further apart, and it lands in just a few drawings. It hardly bounces. A light object is held back by the air. Its drawings stay close together, it wanders sideways, and it takes a long time to land.

SLIDE 03 — Change the shape, never the size  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-03.mp3)
A soft object stretches when it moves fast, and squashes when it hits something. The squash is usually just one drawing. The rule is that the volume never changes. If it gets wider, it gets shorter. If it gets longer, it gets thinner. If you break that rule, the object looks like it is growing.

SLIDE 04 — Down first, then further than life  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-04.mp3)
A reaction drawn at its real size looks stiff on screen. So animators exaggerate. That does not mean distorting everything. It means finding what the action is about, and turning that up. And put an anticipation in front of it. A character that sinks down before it shoots up looks surprised. One that only goes up looks inflated.

SLIDE 05 — If it reads in black, it reads  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-05.mp3)
The audience has about a second to read each pose. Staging means making the idea completely clear. The test is simple. Fill the pose in with black. If you can still tell what the character is doing, the pose is well staged. If you cannot, move the arms and the props out to where there is empty space behind them.

SLIDE 06 — Before you draw  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-06.mp3)
Four exercises, then one shot of your own. Before you draw, answer three questions from memory. Which lands in fewer drawings, a heavy object or a light one? When a ball gets wider, what else happens to it? And what is the silhouette test? Then open the workbook.
