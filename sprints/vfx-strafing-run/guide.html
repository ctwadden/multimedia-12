Shot brief

Hold the barricade. Lose it.

Deliver an 8.5-second shot: a squad holds a rock barricade and fires on an approaching fighter, which strafes them in the last two seconds. Then make the change your director asks for.

  • A three-decision explanation and full credits, including the character’s licence

Industry connection

Crowds and destruction.

Battle scenes are built from a small library of motion-captured actions reused across many characters, then finished with filmed pyrotechnic elements. Crowd artists, FX artists and compositors each own one layer of what you build here.

Knowledge first

Why this works.

Open the theory deck for the narrated version with checkpoint questions. In short:

Reuse, with permission

A downloaded character and motion-captured actions, used within their licence and credited.

Actions and strips

Actions are clips; the NLA editor sequences, repeats and blends them, differently for each trooper.

Tension, then release

Six seconds of waiting make two seconds of attack land. The camera stays with the squad.

A blast is layers

Light, a thrown body, debris, then filmed dirt and fire.

Design principles in the shot

Rhythm: hits three frames apart. Contrast: white armour against dark rock. Emphasis: the camera holds where the damage is.

IN SHORTThree passes; haze and glow; a dirt charge keyed to each hit and masked; a short fireball in Screen; duplicate for seven hits; finish and render.
READ ALOUD

SELECT: beauty, mist and glow passes; Dirt Charge and Charge clips
TOOLS / ROUTE: Render Animation ×3 · Import · Position and Scale keys · Mode · Mask
  1. Render the three passes exactly as in Canyon Dogfight steps 9–10, into strafe/beauty, strafe/mist, strafe/glow (204 frames each).
  2. After Effects: import the three sequences at 24 fps, make the comp from the beauty pass, add the Haze solid with the mist pass as a Luma matte (30%), and the two glow layers in Add (blur 8 at 45%, blur 40 at 22%).
  3. Import Dirt_Charge_01, _05, _09 and Charge_01. For the first hit: drag a Dirt Charge in, start it 2 frames before the hit frame, set its Anchor Point to the base of the dirt (600, 630), then key Position on the blast and Scale so the column is about twice a trooper’s height.
  4. Draw a rectangle mask on the dirt layer that cuts off the clip’s own ground (Feather 14). Add Charge_01 on top: Mode Screen, 70%, same position, 22 frames long.
  5. Repeat for the other six hits (Ctrl+D the pair, slide 3 frames later, move to the next blast). Add the Grade, Vignette and Letterbox layers, then render H.264.

Action by action: what to click (orange boxes), and what you should see

1A dirt-charge layer selected, with Position and Scale keyed.

What went wrong when we built the demo: the clips were positioned in the demo by a script that read each blast’s screen position from Blender. By hand, place the clip on the hit frame and add a key every few frames only if the camera moves.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Anchor Point: The point a layer scales and moves from
Mask: A shape that hides part of a layer
WHYFilmed dirt and fire carry the detail no quick 3D effect can. Position, scale and timing are what make a flat clip belong to a 3D blast.
CHECKEach hit has a dirt column and a short fireball sitting on it; nothing floats, and no clip shows a straight edge.
