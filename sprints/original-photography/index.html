What counts most
The most important evidence is lesson 8. You change your campaign to a new format on your own and explain why. Finishing tutorials, using supports and effort are noticed, but they are recorded separately from achievement.
Rules for photos of people
  • Only photograph people who agree, and check with your teacher that they are cleared on the school’s photo-permission list before any image of them is used in public.
  • Never photograph people in private places (washrooms, change rooms) or anyone who asks you to stop.
  • Every photo in your campaign must be your own original. Record it in your source and consent log.

mm12-26-s03-d02Tue 6 Oct · 70 minutes
Capture for purpose
Editing cannot rescue a blurry or badly exposed photograph. Today you change one camera setting on purpose and watch what it does.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
SettingChange itWhat happens
Aperture (f-number)Smaller number, e.g. f/2.8More light; background becomes blurrier (shallow depth of field)
Shutter speedFaster, e.g. 1/1000 sLess light; freezes movement
ISOHigher, e.g. 1600Brighter; more grainy noise
FramingMove closer, lower or to the sideChanges what is included and what the viewer notices first
One “stop” doubles or halves the light. If you open the aperture one stop, you can use a shutter speed twice as fast for the same brightness.
Produce · 35 minutes
Rotate through the stations in threes: operator, subject, reviewer. Each person shoots their own sequence.
  1. Camera care: strap on, lens cap off, check the battery and card before shooting.
  2. Station A · Framing: shoot the same subject from three positions (eye level, low, close).
  3. Station B · One setting: change only aperture or shutter speed across three shots. Keep everything else the same.
  4. Station C · Steady: shoot one handheld and one braced or on a tripod at a slow shutter speed.
What you should see: On playback, your three Station B shots look clearly different in brightness or blur, and you can say which setting caused it.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Every photo looks the same? You may be in full Auto. Switch the mode dial to A/Av (aperture) or S/Tv (shutter) and change one dial.
  • Photos are blurry? Use a faster shutter speed, brace your elbows, or use the tripod.
  • Using a phone? Tap to focus, then slide the brightness control; note that you changed exposure, not aperture.

Lighting changes meaning
Light decides mood and what detail the viewer can read. Today you photograph one subject under two light arrangements.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
LightLooks likeGood for
Soft (window, cloudy day, diffuser)Gentle shadows with soft edgesFriendly portraits, products
Hard (direct sun, bare lamp)Sharp, dark shadowsDrama, strong shape
Side lightShows texture and formObjects, surfaces, mood
Back lightGlowing edges or silhouetteMystery; separating a subject from the background
Produce · 35 minutes
  1. Choose one subject that fits your brief (an object, a place or a willing person).
  2. Light study 1: soft, even light (near a window, or with a white card bouncing light into the shadows).
  3. Light study 2: a contrasting arrangement, such as side light or back light.
  4. Keep the framing the same so light is the only real difference.
What you should see: Two photos of the same subject with the same framing, where the mood clearly differs.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Face too dark against a bright window? Turn the subject toward the window, or bounce light back with white paper.
  • Colours look orange or blue? Mixed light sources. Switch off one type of light, or use only window light.

mm12-26-s03-d05Fri 9 Oct · 70 minutes
Original capture session
Today you shoot the plan, check focus and clipping on the spot, and reshoot at least one problem.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
The histogram shows brightness from dark (left) to bright (right). A tall spike pressed against either edge means detail is lost (clipped). Zoom in on playback to check focus on the most important part.
Produce · 35 minutes
  1. Shoot your shot list in order. Take several frames of each shot.
  2. After each shot: zoom in to check focus, and check the histogram.
  3. Reshoot at least one problem now, not later.
  4. Copy your files to your class folder before the end of the lesson and name them clearly.
What you should see: At least one in-focus lead image with detail in both the brightest and darkest areas that matter.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Out of focus? Half-press to focus on the subject’s eyes or main edge, then press fully.
  • Sky blown out? Lower the exposure (exposure compensation −1) or change your angle.
  • Someone can’t attend? Use your backup route from lesson 4.

mm12-26-s03-d06Tue 13 Oct · 70 minutes
Select and edit with restraint
An edit can improve a photo or change the truth. Today you choose your photo using the brief and edit it so every change can be undone.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
Reversible editing means the original pixels are never destroyed. In Photoshop:
  • Adjustment layers (Layer › New Adjustment Layer › Levels, Curves or Hue/Saturation) change the look on a separate layer you can switch off.
  • Layer masks hide parts of an adjustment without erasing.
  • Crop with “Delete Cropped Pixels” unticked, so you can re-crop later.
Produce · 35 minutes
  1. Mark your selects on your contact sheet. Choose the one that best serves the brief.
  2. Open it in Photoshop and save it straight away as a .psd master.
  3. Fix exposure and colour with adjustment layers only.
  4. Crop non-destructively for your main format.
  5. Record every edit in your log.
What you should see: In the Layers panel your original photo layer is unchanged, with adjustment layers above it. Switching them off shows the original.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Edits changed the photo layer itself? Undo (Ctrl/Cmd+Z), then use Layer › New Adjustment Layer instead of Image › Adjustments.
  • Mask hides the wrong area? Press X to swap brush colour: black hides, white reveals.

mm12-26-s03-d07Wed 14 Oct · 70 minutes
Compose the original campaign
Now you combine your photograph with type and hierarchy, using what you learned in the Design Studio Explorer and Truck Ad.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
Place your edited master as a Smart Object (Layer › Smart Objects › Convert to Smart Object) so resizing never loses quality. Add type on its own layers. Keep text in the space you planned.
Produce · 35 minutes
  1. Create the campaign document at your main format size.
  2. Place the photo as a Smart Object; add the headline, supporting line and call to action.
  3. Check contrast between text and photo; use a shape or subtle shadow only if needed.
  4. Run a 30-second audience test: show a classmate for 5 seconds. What did they read first? Revise one thing.
What you should see: Layers named clearly (Photo, Headline, CTA). A viewer can state the message after 5 seconds.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Text hard to read? Move it to a plainer part of the photo, raise contrast, or add a soft gradient behind it.
  • Everything looks equally important? Make the headline much bigger than everything else.
Knowledge check: complete the eight-item check your teacher opens today (the Form link comes from your teacher).

mm12-26-s03-d09Fri 16 Oct · 70 minutes
Defence and motion handover
You explain your decisions, look at real creative jobs, and prepare your layers so your campaign can move in Sprint 4.
Retrieve · 5 minutes
Demonstrate and predict · 10 minutes
For motion, the subject and background must be on separate layers. Select › Subject can start a mask; refine the edges by hand. Export layers with File › Export › Layers to Files.
Produce · 35 minutes
  1. Defence (2 minutes with your teacher): show your contact sheet, your edit log and both formats. Explain one capture choice and one edit choice.
  2. Careers: compare two roles, for example photographer, photo editor/retoucher, art director or motion designer.
  3. Motion prep: separate the subject, background and text onto clearly named layers and export them.
What you should see: A folder with separate PNG layers (subject, background, headline) that line up when stacked.
Stuck? Try this:
  • Hole behind the subject? Extend the background by hand, or shoot a clean background plate next time. Note any content-aware fill in your ethics log.
Adapt or critique · 15 minutes
Post your campaign to the Student OS class gallery (share with class) and leave one specific comment on two classmates’ work.
