Industry connection

Virtual production, at desk scale.

Film and TV now build worlds in 3D and put actors into them, on LED stages or with green screen. Matchmove artists track cameras, compositors key and integrate, environment artists build the worlds. Solo creators like Drifting Walnut and Ian Hubert do all three jobs themselves in Blender.

The three stages you learn here (World, Plate, Integrate) are the same ones a studio pipeline uses, and the same checks apply: solve error, clean matte, contact and matched light.

Knowledge first

Why a composite looks real (or fake).

Parallax and camera tracking

When a camera moves, near points shift more than far points. Tracking markers at different depths let Blender calculate the camera’s path. The solve error (in pixels) tells you how well it worked: under 1 is the target.

Keying and mattes

A keyer turns one colour transparent. The matte is the black-and-white map of what stays. A garbage matte removes things the keyer cannot know are unwanted.

Scale and grounding

A filmed person is a fixed size, so the world must be in real metres. Feet must meet the ground on every frame; if the person moves nearer or further, the footage must move in depth with them.

Light and colour match

Light direction, shadow direction, brightness and colour temperature must agree between the person and the world. Decide the light before you shoot.

Design principles in the shot

Emphasis: the person is the sharpest, most detailed thing. Depth: haze and overlapping shapes. Balance: person and largest prop on opposite thirds. Unity: one light colour across everything.

08

Solve the camera and get the error under 1 pixel

IN SHORTKeyframes A/B far apart; solve; clean worst tracks; re-solve; refine focal; under 1 px.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: Shot_YourName.blend
SELECT: Solve tab
TOOLS / ROUTE: Solve › Keyframe A / B · Refine Focal Length · Solve Camera Motion · Clean Up › Clean Tracks
  1. Toolbar › Solve: turn off automatic Keyframe selection and set Keyframe A and B to two frames far apart where the camera has clearly moved (the demo used 20 and 100).
  2. Click Solve Camera Motion. Read the Solve error at the top of the editor.
  3. If it is above 1 px: Clean Up › Clean Tracks with Error about 3, delete the selected tracks, and solve again. Repeat with a lower Error.
  4. Tick Refine › Focal Length and solve once more. The lens should settle near a sensible value (the demo: 27.7 mm).
  5. Aim for under 1.0 px with at least 8 tracks on both keyframes.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Solve: Calculating the camera’s position for every frame
Solve error: Average tracking error in pixels; lower is better
WHYThe solve error is the average distance, in pixels, between where markers are and where the solved camera says they should be. Under 1 px, CG objects stay locked to the footage.
CHECKA solve error under 1.0 px, a believable focal length, and markers still spread across the frame.
FIXError stays high? A few bad tracks cause most of it: delete the worst and re-solve. Focal length runs away to a huge number? Your markers are too flat: add markers at other depths.
WORKED EXAMPLE · DIFFERENT OBJECTThe demo went 1.55 → 1.18 → 0.96 → 0.81 px as the worst tracks were removed and the focal length refined.
STRETCH (AFTER THE CHECK PASSES)Open the Graph view in the Clip Editor and find the tracks that spike.

Say or write your answers, then check them against your file.
IN SHORTLight screen, then subject; 2 m from the screen; feet in frame; markers at depths or lock the tripod; two takes.
READ ALOUD
DOCUMENT: Classroom green screen, LED lights, camera or phone
SELECT: Your plate
TOOLS / ROUTE: Your shoot rules from step 6
  1. Light the screen evenly first. Then light yourself separately, from the direction of your world’s sun.
  2. Stand at least 2 m from the screen to avoid green spill. Keep your feet in frame.
  3. For a moving camera: tape markers on the screen and on stands or boxes at different depths, and move slowly. For a first shot, a locked-off tripod is fine and needs no tracking.
  4. Record 10 seconds of action that fits your story sentence. Do two takes.
  5. Copy the files into your project folder straight away and name them.
STEP CHECKLIST
GLOSSARY
Spill: Green light bouncing from the screen onto the subject
Locked-off: A camera that does not move
WHYMost compositing problems are created on set. Even screen light, separation from the screen and matched light direction are worth more than any software fix.

FIXShadows on the screen? Move further from it. Green on your skin? Step forward and add a backlight.

TOOLS / ROUTE: Grounded Footage add-on

GLOSSARY

WHYOn a fixed card, a person walking away only slides up the frame and seems to float. Moving the card to the foot’s depth each frame gives true size and contact. This is the fix Ian Hubert describes.
CHECKYourself in your world, grounded through the whole clip, with a matching shadow.
FIXFeet slide when you change foot? Re-place the Foot marker at each step. Jittery depth? Raise Smoothing in the add-on.
