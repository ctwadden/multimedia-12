Industry connection

This is how an establishing shot is built.

Animation and VFX studios build scenes in layers exactly like this: block-out, modelling, texturing (often with free libraries like ambientCG and Poly Haven), lighting, effects (particles and fluids), animation and compositing. Each layer can be handed to a different artist, which is why clean names and a tidy project folder matter.

Blender is free and used by independent studios, YouTube creators and on award-winning films. Solo creators like Ian Hubert and Drifting Walnut make whole shots alone with these same tools, which is where this unit is heading.

Knowledge first

What each layer of a scene does.

3D space and modelling

Objects have location, rotation and scale on X (red), Y (green) and Z (blue). Edit Mode shapes vertices, edges and faces; modifiers (Bevel, Subdivision, Displace) add detail without destroying the original.

Materials, UVs and textures

A UV map unfolds a model flat so an image can wrap onto it. Colour, roughness and normal maps together make a surface read as wood, brick or tile. Emission makes a surface glow.

Weight painting and particles

Weights (0 blue to 1 red) stored on vertices can control where particles grow, and later how a character bends. Particle systems create thousands of copies (trees, snowflakes) from one setting.

Simulation

Smoke is a fluid simulation inside a domain box, calculated (baked) once and then played back.

Animation and finishing

Keyframes save values at moments in time; the Graph Editor controls easing. The compositor adds glow and colour after rendering.

Design principles in the shot

Emphasis: the warm windows are the brightest thing. Contrast: warm light against cool snow and sky. Balance and thirds: cottage and snowman on opposite thirds. Movement: smoke rising and snow falling lead the eye through the frame.

Orbit, pan and zoom change your view of the scene. Move, rotate and scale change an object. X, Y and Z are the three directions in 3D space; Z is up in this project. 06

UV-unwrap the cottage and add real textures

How a texture wraps around a model

UV coordinates are the model’s flat address map for an image. Mapping changes the coordinates sent to each texture, controlling repetition and alignment. Colour gives appearance, roughness controls reflections, and a normal map changes apparent fine surface direction without changing the silhouette. Colour images use sRGB; roughness and normal images use Non-Color because their pixels represent data. All three maps need the same coordinates to stay aligned.

Visible glow and illumination

Emission makes a surface appear luminous. A light controls illumination on nearby objects. Our opaque cottage uses a separate Area light outside each window to approximate spill; increasing an interior lamp cannot make it shine through an opaque wall. World lighting surrounds the scene, while a Sun light supplies one consistent direction. Warm local lights against cooler surroundings help the viewer locate the cottage.

A camera and a render

The camera defines the final frame. Its lens affects the field of view; its transform sets position and direction. Thirds guides help compare composition without changing the render. Cycles calculates lighting with sampled rays; more samples can reduce noise but take longer. Denoising reduces remaining noise. AgX converts the render’s large brightness range for the display. Painting height rather than colour

A height map stores surface height as brightness. With Midlevel 1, white stays at the original height, black moves down by the displacement strength, and grey moves down partway. The Displace modifier needs enough vertices to form those depressions. The unconnected image node identifies our paint target; it must not replace the snow’s Base Color. Save the painted image as well as the .blend file, because an external image has its own unsaved state.

Make smoke rise from the chimney

A smoke source, a domain and a cache

The flow source adds smoke. The domain is the volume in which Blender simulates it; smoke outside that volume cannot continue. Resolution controls the simulation grid, affecting detail and memory. A volume material describes the smoke’s appearance. Baking stores the calculated simulation on disk. Changing the source, domain or forces invalidates that result, so free the old cache and bake again before judging the change.

Animate with keyframes: a camera push-in and flickering lanterns

Keyframes and interpolation

A keyframe stores a property’s value at a particular frame. Interpolation determines the values between keys: Linear makes a constant rate; Bezier can ease into and out of movement. At 24 frames per second, 96 frames produce four seconds. Key only the properties you intend to change. A camera Location key moves the camera, while a light Power key changes illumination; the correct selection and editor context determine which property is recorded.

Render guide: make it believable, and make it finish

Believable comes from the scene, not the sample count. Check these before you touch a render setting.

What to checkWhat to do in this sceneWhy it reads as real
One main lightThe Moon is the key light. Keep the World dim. Lanterns and window lights are accents.Real places have one dominant light and clear shadows.
Warm against coolWarm windows and lanterns, cool blue snow and sky.Colour contrast gives depth and tells the eye where to look.
SurfacesColour, roughness and normal maps on wood, brick and roof. Bevel hard edges.Nothing real is perfectly flat or perfectly sharp.
Glow that lights thingsAn Emission surface glows but hardly lights its surroundings. Add a real light beside it, as you did for the windows.Light spilling on the snow is what sells the window.
Things you must seeSnowflakes need their own bright material, or they vanish against the yard.An effect that is technically there but invisible is not there.
View transformColor Management: AgX.Bright windows roll off gently instead of burning to flat white.

Efficient means spending time only where it shows.

SettingWhereUse
Resolution %Output Properties › Format50 % for every test: about a quarter of the time. 100 % only for the final.
Noise Threshold and Max SamplesRender Properties › Sampling › Render0.05 and 64 here. Cycles stops early on parts of the picture that are already clean.
DenoiseRender Properties › Sampling › RenderOn. It removes the remaining grain, so you need far fewer samples.
Time LimitRender Properties › Sampling › RenderSet a cap per frame when you have a fixed amount of class time.
DeviceRender Properties › Device; Edit › Preferences › System › Cycles Render DevicesGPU Compute is usually faster if the computer has a supported graphics card. CPU always works.
Max BouncesRender Properties › Light PathsLower values render faster and slightly darker. Try 4 total for tests.
Persistent DataRender Properties › Performance › Final RenderFor animations: Blender keeps the scene loaded between frames instead of rebuilding it each time.
Bake firstParticle and smoke Cache panelsBake snow and smoke before rendering so every frame reads a saved result.
PNG frames, then a movieOutput Properties › OutputIf a render stops at frame 60 you keep 59 frames. A movie file would be lost.

Budget before you start. Render one frame, read its time in the render window, and multiply by 96. If that is longer than you have, lower Resolution % or Max Samples and test again.

The classroom PCs have not been timed, so measure your own.
