RECORDING SCRIPT: read each slide in your own voice, one MP3 per slide.

SLIDE 01 — A scene is built in layers  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-01.mp3)
The cottage and snowman are our guided example. First make a saved still: model, surface, light and frame it. Then extend the same scene into a four-second shot with snow, smoke and a camera move. The director’s card later changes the communication problem.

SLIDE 02 — Each editor has a job  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-02.mp3)
The 3D Viewport shows the scene. The Outliner lists objects and shows selection. Properties contains settings; the Timeline shows time. A workspace rearranges these editors. Changing a workspace does not create a new scene. Blender sends shortcuts to the editor under your pointer.

SLIDE 03 — Look around without moving objects  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-03.mp3)
Orbit changes your viewing angle, pan shifts your view and zoom changes its size. These controls do not change an object’s Location. Move, rotate and scale do change the object. Orthographic view removes perspective size changes, helping you line up a row of vertices.

SLIDE 04 — Object Mode and Edit Mode  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-04.mp3)
A mesh has vertices, edges and faces. Object Mode transforms the complete object. Edit Mode changes the mesh inside it. Choose Vertex, Edge or Face Select before selecting. The orange selection identifies what will change; the active object is the last selected object.

SLIDE 05 — Dimensions and applied scale  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-05.mp3)
Dimensions reports an object’s size. Scale multiplies the size of its underlying mesh. Apply Scale keeps the visible size and resets Scale to one. A bevel measured in metres then behaves predictably. In this project Z is up; typed metre values help the door, car and snowman fit together.

SLIDE 06 — Detail starts with a selected face  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-06.mp3)
Inset makes a smaller face inside a face. Extrude extends selected geometry; a negative distance here recesses the centre. A bevel adds a narrow surface at an edge to catch light. Select the intended face or object before applying any of these changes.

SLIDE 07 — A material describes a surface  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-07.mp3)
Base Color describes the surface colour. Roughness controls how broad its reflections are. Shade Smooth changes shading across faces; it does not add geometry. Assign Snow, Coal and Carrot to the separate snowman pieces before Join so the joined object keeps their material slots.

SLIDE 08 — UV coordinates are image addresses  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-08.mp3)
A UV map tells Blender which part of a flat image belongs at each point on a mesh. Cube Projection suits our box-like cottage. Mapping changes the coordinates sent to textures. Linking UV to Mapping and Mapping to each image keeps the images aligned when their repeat scale changes.

SLIDE 09 — Image pixels can be colour or data  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-09.mp3)
A colour map feeds Base Color and uses sRGB. Roughness and NormalGL maps contain data and use Non-Color. A Normal Map node converts the normal image before it reaches the shader’s Normal input. It changes fine surface shading; it does not change the silhouette.

SLIDE 10 — Visible glow and light spill  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-10.mp3)
Emission makes the window surface appear luminous. A separate light illuminates nearby snow. Our closed cottage uses Area lights just outside the windows as a taught approximation of spill. An interior lamp cannot shine through an opaque wall. Cool surroundings and warm local lights guide attention.

SLIDE 11 — Move an assembly through its parent  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-11.mp3)
Append copies objects from another blend file. Parenting the car parts to a root Empty lets one transform move the whole car. Object Keep Transform preserves their visible positions while parenting. Packing and reopening checks supported external assets. A smoke cache still needs its folder.

SLIDE 12 — The camera defines the deliverable  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-12.mp3)
The viewport is your working view; the camera defines the rendered image. The lens sets the field of view. Compare a wider and a longer lens while maintaining subject size. Thirds guides are visual aids and do not render. Leave room for the later smoke, then disable Camera to View to avoid accidental reframing.

SLIDE 13 — Measure before a larger render  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-13.mp3)
Cycles samples light paths. More samples can reduce noise but take longer; denoising reduces remaining noise. AgX maps a wide brightness range to the display. Use a 50 percent test, then measure a representative full-resolution frame. The classroom PC’s time must be measured; a Mac time is not a PC benchmark.

SLIDE 14 — Paint height rather than colour  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-14.mp3)
With Midlevel 1, white stays at the original height, black moves down by the displacement strength and grey moves down partway. The mesh needs enough vertices to form those depressions. The unconnected image node identifies the paint target. Save the painted PNG as well as the blend, then reopen to check both.

SLIDE 15 — Weights control where trees appear  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-15.mp3)
A vertex group stores a weight at each vertex: blue is zero and red is one. Binding Forest to the particle Density setting controls where trees can originate. It does not paint their material. Instances reuse a source mesh. Removing weight from one area redistributes this system’s fixed tree count elsewhere.

SLIDE 16 — Particles need time to fill the shot  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-16.mp3)
The emitter produces particles; the Snowflake object defines their appearance. Lifetime, velocity, gravity and wind determine their movement. Emission starts before frame 1 so snow is already present at the opening. Bake the particle cache and test playback after reopening; a single still cannot prove moving snow.

SLIDE 17 — Smoke belongs inside its domain  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-17.mp3)
The flow source adds smoke; the domain is the volume where Blender simulates it. Resolution controls grid detail and memory. Baking stores the calculation on disk. Changed flow, wind or domain settings require freeing the old cache and rebaking. Increasing resolution cannot fix a source outside the domain.

SLIDE 18 — Key the property you intend to change  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-18.mp3)
A keyframe stores a value at a frame. Interpolation determines values between keys; Bezier can ease a movement. In the demonstrated keymap, viewport K opens the keying menu. Choose Location for the camera. Hovering over a light’s Power and pressing I keys that property. At 24 fps, 96 frames gives four seconds.

SLIDE 19 — Compare the same frame  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-19.mp3)
The compositor processes the render through nodes. Bloom spreads bright light; Color Balance adjusts tonal colour. These effects cannot repair missing geometry or a bad cache. Mute both finishing nodes and render again, then restore them and render the same frame. Keep window colour and snow detail readable.

SLIDE 20 — Rendering and encoding are separate  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-20.mp3)
Rendering creates numbered PNG frames. Encoding combines them into a movie. A stopped render keeps completed frames for recovery. Our movie uses an MP4 container with H.264 video at 24 fps. Watch the exported clip and inspect every fifth frame; neither a successful command nor a sample sheet proves all intervening frames are clean.

SLIDE 21 — The director’s card is a new problem  (save your recording as audio/theory/slide-21.mp3)
Identify the audience and intended feeling, sketch two distinct responses, then choose and defend your own approach. Test one meaningful change, gather audience feedback and preserve a justified revision. Supports may clarify tools or help you explain. They must leave the assessed concept, composition and settings yours.
